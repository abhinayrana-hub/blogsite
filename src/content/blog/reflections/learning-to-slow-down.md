We confuse busyness with momentum, and momentum with progress.

A lot of the work I do is creative, strategic and uncertain. That means the most useful thing is not to sprint, but to stay attentive long enough to see what actually matters.

## Why slowness feels uncomfortable

The modern workday rewards speed. We optimize for output, response time and immediate feedback. These are useful signals, but they can also become a default rhythm.

What I am learning is that the best work often comes from a slower loop: thinking, observing, trying, refining.

## A small discipline

I try to leave space in my day for the work that does not immediately look productive.

That includes reading, wandering, journaling and pausing before deciding on the next action. Those moments are not idle. They are where clarity forms.

Slow work is not passive work. It is attention under control.
